# Levitating Knife — Universal Magnetic Puck

A small wall-mounted magnetic knife holder, designed so the wood backing is **invisible behind the knife**. Each knife gets one or more tiny 25 mm pucks hidden behind its bolster/heel, so the blade looks like it's floating on the wall.

See [assembly-diagram.svg](./assembly-diagram.svg) for an exploded view of all three mount options on one page.

---

## TL;DR

- **One universal part.** 25 mm diameter × 12 mm thick hardwood puck with a ring magnet inside and a leather face.
- **~4–5 kg pull per puck.** Use more pucks for heavier knives (1 puck for a paring knife, up to 4 for a 300 mm yanagiba).
- **Three mounts, same puck body**: VHB tape, wood dowel, or M4 screw.
- **Hand-tool buildable** with a 20 mm Forstner bit, a 4.5 mm twist bit, and (per mount) a 10 mm or 9 mm Forstner.

## Puck specification

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Outer diameter | **25 mm** |
| Thickness | **12 mm** wood + 1 mm leather = **13 mm** installed |
| Front magnet pocket | 20 mm Ø × 5 mm deep (drilled from front) |
| Front wall (wood between magnet and knife) | 2 mm |
| Center through-hole | 4.5 mm Ø (full depth) |
| Front facing | 1 mm veg-tan leather disc, Ø25 mm, contact-cemented |
| Wood species | Walnut, white oak, maple, or any dense hardwood |

**Magnet (single SKU, enables all three mounts):**
N52 neodymium **ring magnet**, **20 mm OD × 5 mm thick × 4.5 mm ID**, axially magnetized, Ni-Cu-Ni plated.
Raw pull on thick steel ≈ 7–8 kg. Real pull through 2 mm wood + 1 mm leather onto a typical ~2 mm knife blade ≈ **4–5 kg**.

### Stacking guide (how many pucks per knife)

| Knife class | Examples | Pucks |
|---|---|---|
| Light paring / petty | petty 120 mm, small utility | 1 |
| Standard daily | nakiri 165, santoku 180, gyuto 210, deba 165 | 2 |
| Large | gyuto 240–270, sujihiki 240 | 3 |
| Heavy / long | gyuto 300, sujihiki 270–300, yanagiba 270–300, deba 210 | 4 |

Space pucks along the spine/heel side of the blade, 30–50 mm apart, roughly centered on the blade's length.

## Tools

Core (all builds):
- 20 mm Forstner bit
- 4.5 mm twist bit
- Drill press (strongly preferred) or hand drill with depth collar
- 25 mm hole saw **or** a file/rasp to round 25×25 mm stock
- Bench vise / clamp
- Utility knife (leather)
- Sandpaper 120 / 220 / 400

Per mount:
- Mount B: 10 mm Forstner or brad-point bit
- Mount C: 9 mm Forstner bit, Phillips or Torx driver (matching the screw head)

Optional: danish oil or wax for finishing the wood before leather is glued on.

## Assembly — puck body (common to all three mounts)

1. **Prepare the blank.** Start with a Ø25 mm × 12 mm hardwood disc. If you only have square stock, clamp a 25×25×12 mm blank in a vise, mark the center on both faces, and round with a 25 mm hole saw (use a sacrificial board) or file/rasp + sandpaper.
2. **Mark centers.** On both the front and back faces, mark dead center with a punch — use the [puck-drilling-template.svg](./puck-drilling-template.svg) if you want a printed guide.
3. **Drill the through-hole first.** With a 4.5 mm twist bit, drill straight through from the front. This gives every later drill a natural pilot and keeps concentricity.
4. **Drill the magnet pocket.** With a 20 mm Forstner bit from the front face, drill **5 mm deep**. A depth collar or piece of tape on the bit shaft prevents going too deep. You should be left with 2 mm of wood between the pocket floor and the front face — that front wall is critical for pull strength; don't make it thinner.
5. **Test-fit the magnet.** The N52 ring magnet should drop in with slight wiggle room. If it's tight, do not force it — sand the pocket wall with a rolled bit of 220 paper.
6. **Epoxy the magnet in.** Mix a drop of 5-min epoxy, apply a thin film to the magnet's edge and the pocket side wall, press in so the magnet sits flush or 0.1 mm below the wood's front rim. Wipe squeeze-out with a toothpick. Let cure 30 min.
7. **Finish the wood (optional).** Light sand to 400 grit, one wipe of danish oil on the outer cylinder and back face. Keep oil **out of the magnet pocket rim** — it can interfere with leather glue-up.
8. **Glue the leather face.** Cut a Ø25 mm disc from 1 mm veg-tan leather (a 25 mm hole punch or a knife around a coin works). Contact-cement both the leather back and the puck's front face, let tack off 3–5 min, press together firmly with a clamp or heavy book for 10 min. Trim any overhang with a sharp blade.

The puck body is now complete. Pick a mount.

## Mount A — VHB tape (easiest, low commitment)

Best for: smooth painted drywall, tile, glass splashbacks, cabinet sides.
Worst for: textured paint, wallpaper, flaky surfaces.

1. Clean both surfaces (puck back + wall spot) with isopropyl alcohol. Let dry.
2. Cut a ~20 × 20 mm square of 3M VHB 4941. Peel one liner, stick to the back of the puck (centered — the 4.5 mm hole is irrelevant; VHB bridges it fine).
3. Peel the second liner, press the puck onto the wall. **Press hard for 20 seconds.** VHB develops ~50% strength in 20 min, full strength in 72 h.
4. Wait 24 h before hanging a knife. Then test with a knife *that you don't mind dropping* before trusting it with your good blade.

If the puck pops off within 24 h, the wall surface is unsuitable — switch to Mount C.

## Mount B — Hidden wood panel with dowels (cleanest look)

This is the "floating" setup from your sketch. The wall panel is whatever shape and wood you like — it becomes a subtle feature strip. Pucks friction-fit onto short dowels glued into the panel, so there's nothing visible on the front of the panel except the pucks (which themselves disappear behind the knives).

**Wall panel prep:**

1. Cut the back panel — minimum 15 mm thick hardwood or good-quality plywood. Size and shape per your knife layout; the reference photo spaces knives ~60 mm apart horizontally and staggers them vertically.
2. Plan the puck positions. Each knife takes 1–4 pucks along the blade's spine. See [wall-panel-drilling-guide.svg](./wall-panel-drilling-guide.svg).
3. Drill 10 mm dowel holes in the panel, **5–8 mm deep**. Use a drill press and depth stop.
4. Mount the panel to the wall — French cleat, construction adhesive (for permanent), or screws through the panel into studs. The panel itself is not dimensioned by this design; it's whatever matches your kitchen.

**Puck back prep (Mount B):**

1. On the back face of the completed puck, drill a **10 mm Ø × 5 mm deep** Forstner pocket, centered (the pilot of the Forstner lands in the 4.5 mm through-hole you drilled in step 3 of the puck assembly).
2. Light sand the inside of this socket — it should be a **firm friction fit** on the dowel, requiring ~1–2 kg of pull to remove.

**Assemble:**

1. Cut 10 mm long pieces of 10 mm dowel.
2. Glue each dowel into its wall-panel hole with wood glue. Leave ~5 mm projecting.
3. Let glue cure overnight.
4. Press each puck onto its dowel. Done.

If the fit is loose, dip the dowel in glue before the puck — they become permanent but still discreet. If too tight, sand a thou off the dowel.

## Mount C — M4 screw (most secure, survives earthquakes and enthusiastic toddlers)

Best for: rental walls where you're okay with one small screw hole per puck; situations where VHB is unreliable; users who want zero worry.

**Puck back prep (Mount C):**

1. On the back face, drill a **9 mm Ø × 2 mm deep countersink** with a 9 mm Forstner, centered on the existing 4.5 mm through-hole. This recess catches the screw head flush with the back surface.
2. Dry-fit an M4 × 30 mm countersunk wood screw through the puck: the head should sit flush with the back face, the shaft should extend ~18 mm past the back. If the head sits proud, deepen the countersink 0.5 mm at a time.

**Install:**

1. Mark the wall at each puck's position.
2. **If hitting drywall only:** pre-drill a 5 mm hole, push in a plastic expansion anchor rated for M4.
   **If hitting a stud or wood panel:** pre-drill a 3 mm pilot hole.
3. Feed the screw through the puck (head seated in the countersink, shaft protruding through the magnet's 4.5 mm bore — steel in the magnetic circuit actually boosts the pull slightly).
4. Drive the screw in until the puck is snug against the wall. **Do not overtighten** — the wood around the countersink will crack. Stop at "firm, no wobble".

If the puck spins freely when you try to tighten it, the anchor has failed — remove, step up anchor size, or relocate to a stud.

## Safety notes

- **N52 magnets pinch hard.** A single 20×5 mm ring magnet can bite skin between itself and another magnet or any ferrous steel. Keep pucks separated by ≥50 mm in storage.
- **Pacemakers / implanted electronics:** keep pucks >30 cm from these devices.
- **Children:** swallowed neodymium magnets are a medical emergency. Treat loose magnets like button batteries — out of reach.
- **Knife retention is passive.** Earthquake, heavy door slam, or a knife bumped hard may dislodge a blade. Don't mount over a baby's crib, dining chair, or pet bed. A knee-height or chest-height mount over counter is ideal.
- **Test every build** with a cheap knife before trusting the puck(s) with an expensive blade.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Knife slides down slowly | Not enough magnet per knife weight | Add another puck |
| Knife falls off immediately | Front wall too thick (>3 mm), or leather too thick | Sand front face thinner, or replace leather with 0.5 mm |
| Blade gets scratched over time | Leather worn through | Replace leather disc |
| VHB puck pops off wall | Surface not clean / textured / flaky paint | Clean with IPA and retry; if still failing, switch to Mount C |
| Dowel puck pulls off with the knife | Friction fit too loose | Dip dowel in wood glue before pressing puck on |
| Screw puck spins when tightening | Wall anchor stripped or missing | Pull out, install larger anchor, or relocate to a stud |
| Puck cracks when tightening screw | Overtightened | Back off ¼ turn; cracked pucks still work — epoxy fix |
| Magnet pops out of pocket | Epoxy failure or pocket oversized | Re-epoxy with thin shim of paper around magnet edge |
